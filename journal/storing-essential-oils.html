How to store essential oils so they actually last

Light, heat, and air are what age an oil - not time alone. Get those three under control and most oils will happily outlive their reputation.

Guides July 2026 5 min read

People often ask me how long an essential oil "lasts", as though there is a single number. There is not. An oil left on a sunny windowsill can turn in a few months, while the same oil kept properly can be beautiful for years. The date on the bottle matters far less than how you treat it. Three things do the ageing - light, heat, and air - so storing oils well is really just a matter of denying them all three.

1. Keep them in the dark

Ultraviolet light breaks down the delicate aromatic compounds that give an oil its scent and its character. This is exactly why quality oils are sold in amber or dark glass rather than clear bottles - the dark glass is doing a job, not just looking the part. Keep oils in their original coloured bottles, and keep those bottles somewhere dark: a closed cupboard or drawer is perfect. Never leave them sitting out in daylight.

2. Keep them cool - and steady

Heat speeds up every reaction that spoils an oil, and swings between hot and cold are worse still. The single most common storage mistake I see is the bathroom shelf: it looks convenient, but a bathroom is one of the warmest, most humid, most temperature-swung rooms in the house. Choose a cool, stable spot instead. A cupboard in an interior room is ideal.

The fridge is optional but genuinely helps for the most fragile oils - the citruses especially. If you do refrigerate, let the bottle come back to room temperature before opening, so condensation does not form inside and introduce water.

3. Keep the air out

Oxygen is the quiet enemy. Every time you open a bottle, a little air gets in, and oxidation slowly begins. Two simple habits keep it in check: close caps firmly straight after use, and as a bottle empties, decant what is left into a smaller bottle so there is less air sitting above the oil. A half-empty large bottle ages far faster than a full small one.

One more detail - if your oils came with rubber dropper inserts, do not store them long-term with the dropper in place. Concentrated oils degrade rubber over time, which can taint the oil and gum up the dropper. Use the solid cap for storage and the dropper only when you are dispensing.

3 enemies
Light, heat, and air. Beat all three - dark glass, a cool steady cupboard, tight caps and small bottles - and shelf life stops being a worry.

Not all oils age at the same speed

It helps to know which of your oils are sprinters and which are marathoners:

  • Store carefully, use sooner: citrus oils (bergamot, sweet orange, lemon, lime) and the conifers age fastest. They are rich in compounds that oxidise readily, so buy them in sensible amounts and keep them coolest.
  • Comfortably stable: most florals, herbs and mints sit happily for a couple of years or more when stored well.
  • Actually improve with age: a few materials - patchouli, sandalwood, vetiver, and many resins - deepen and round out over years, like a good spirit. These are the tortoises, and they win.

Why it is worth the bother

This is not only about protecting the scent. An oxidised oil is more likely to irritate skin than a fresh one, so good storage is a small safety habit as much as a quality one. My last tip is the easiest of all: write the date you opened each bottle on the label. It takes a second, and it turns "I think this is a couple of years old" into something you actually know.
